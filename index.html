The Mathematical Index

Weight (kg) / [Height (m)]²

Established by Adolphe Quetelet, BMI serves as a universal screening surrogate. While simple to track, its metabolic relevance relies on clinical context rather than the raw number alone.

Ethnic Threshold Adjustments

A critical limitation of the standard WHO model is the ethnic variation in body fat distribution. Asian populations exhibit significantly higher visceral adiposity and cardiovascular risk at lower BMI values. Clinical guidelines now recommend lower thresholds for Type 2 Diabetes screening.

Screening Alert

Asian-American Threshold

Screening for T2D is clinically recommended starting at a BMI of 23.0 kg/m².

Obesity Definition

South Asian Adjustment

The obesity threshold is lowered to 25.0 kg/m², reflecting high metabolic risk from visceral fat.

The BMI Blind Spots

Beyond BMI: ABCD Framework

The American Association of Clinical Endocrinology defines Obesity as Adiposity-Based Chronic Disease (ABCD), focusing on functional complications.

Biomedical Complications

  • ✦ Type 2 Diabetes & Insulin Resistance
  • ✦ Dyslipidemia & Hypertension
  • ✦ MASLD / NAFLD (Liver Adiposity)

Biomechanical Complications

  • ✦ Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)
  • ✦ Osteoarthritis & Joint Strain
  • ✦ GERD & Immobility Challenges

Advanced Anthropometric Tools

Pairing BMI with direct measures for precise metabolic risk assessment.

Metabolic Marker

Waist Circumference

The gold standard for abdominal obesity assessment. Thresholds vary by population (e.g., 88cm in US women, 102cm in US men).

Emerging Metric

Waist-to-Height Ratio

Superior to BMI for cardiometabolic prediction. A universal boundary of WHtR ≥ 0.5 indicates elevated risk.

Biometric Data

VAT & HOMA-IR

Direct measurement of Visceral Adipose Tissue and Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance.
